# ANOM Série E — French administrative papers, 18th–19th century

Colonial personnel dossiers from the Archives nationales d'Outre-Mer. Point the
plugin's **Collection notes** preference at this file, or copy it as a starting
point for another collection.

Everything here is additive: it makes the built-in policy concrete for this
material. It should not contradict it.

## Dates

- Dates appear as `3 juin 1780`, and are recorded that way. The place and the
  connecting words are not part of the date: `Versailles le 30 avril 1780`,
  `Du 30 avril 1780` and `ce 30 avril 1780` all record as `30 avril 1780`.
- **Republican dates stay in the Republican calendar** — `16 frimaire an 11`,
  not `1802-12-07`. Republican years do not align with Gregorian ones, so a
  converted year is frequently wrong even when the arithmetic is right.
- Archaic and abbreviated forms are kept as written: `7bre` for *septembre*,
  `may` for *mai*. These are the clerk's spelling, not errors.
- Dates do not run in order within a dossier, and nothing should assume they
  do. A date jump supports a boundary; a date sequence proves nothing.

## Document types in this material

The *lettre*; the *minute*, a retained draft reply, usually heavily struck
through with insertions in the margin; the *mémoire*; the *rapport*. Which of
these becomes a `type` value depends on the vocabulary the project already
uses.

A draft reply and the letter it answers are separate documents, and the reply
is often filed *before* the letter.

## Wrappers

A personnel dossier usually opens with one or two wrappers — a modern
descriptive label, then the archival cover — before the first document. Neither
is a document.

## Surrogates

This material is microfilm, not direct photography. Every frame looks the same,
so cues about the photograph itself — background, lighting, camera distance —
carry no information, and everything rests on paper, hand, ink and layout.

Many frames show a slice of the neighbouring leaf at the edge, because the
leaves were filmed stacked. That is a capture artifact, not a second document
and not a boundary.

## Identity

Docket numbers — `N° 171`, `N° 897` — and archival foliation are the most
legible things on the page, and read clearly even downscaled. They are good
evidence of both boundary and identity.

Marginal apostilles (`approuvé`, `refusé`) belong to the document they annotate.

Duplicates are normal: two copies of a service record, or the same letter sent
to two recipients on one day, are distinct physical documents and each becomes
its own item.
